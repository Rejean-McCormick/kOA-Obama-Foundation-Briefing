# 04 — Democratic Capacity and Non-Domination

Power is not only money, law, or formal authority. It is also the ability to organize.

A large institution can retain specialized knowledge, coordinate thousands of people, hire expertise, route information, enforce process, track work, preserve records, train replacements, and communicate at scale.

A community may possess equally valuable local knowledge and intelligence but lack the infrastructure needed to convert it into coordinated action.

The long-term purpose of kOA is to reduce part of this organizational-capacity gap.

## Empowerment without a new center

The system is designed around a tension:

> **Increase collective capacity without making the infrastructure itself the new sovereign.**

Recurring design goals include local/offline operation, self-hosting, explicit authority boundaries, portability, forkability, replaceable external services, visible provenance, contestability, advisory expertise rather than expert sovereignty, AI assistance without final civic authority, and preserved attribution.

## Founder-independence

The architecture fails its own social ambition if participation simply creates a new dependency on its founder.

A mature trajectory is:

```text
founder-driven → protocolized → operated by others → federated
```

The final objective is a capability that can continue, evolve, and replicate without a mandatory human center.
